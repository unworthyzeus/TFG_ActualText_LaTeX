\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

This chapter defines the reproducible prediction pipeline: the CKM dataset
contract, receiver mask, city holdout split, pixel weighted metrics, calibrated
priors, and \textsc{HARP-Net CKM} residual prediction for channel attenuation,
delay spread, and angular spread. For readability, the implementation is
grouped into map support quantities, calibrated prior maps and the residual
neural model. This grouping is a guide to the data flow, not a strict
preprocessing checklist: some quantities are computed once from geometry, some
are fitted on the training cities, and some are learned by the neural residual
model. Appendix~\ref{app:detailed_attempt_log} keeps the development history;
this chapter keeps the final pipeline and reproduction details.

The ordering is important. The masks, distances and morphology maps are
deterministic quantities derived from the topology and transmitter height. The
prior coefficients are fitted once on the training cities and then frozen. The
neural model therefore does not have to rediscover the full propagation law from
pixels; it receives a structured set of computed maps and learns only bounded
local corrections. In this chapter, the word \emph{feature} refers to an input
map supplied to the model. Fitted constants, regression weights and checkpoint
selection settings are parameters of the prior or training protocol, not extra
neural network features.

\section{Dataset and split}
The data source is \texttt{CKM\_Dataset\_270326.h5}, a project HDF5 version of
the CKM radio map data associated with the public path loss and ToA radio map
dataset of Yapar et al.~\cite{dataset2212}. At dataset level, it contains
16180 ray traced urban samples from 66 real city scenarios. The fields used by
the final pipeline are the building topology map, the CKM target stored as
\texttt{path\_loss}, the LoS/NLoS support mask, the UAV transmitter height
\texttt{uav\_height}, \texttt{delay\_spread}, and
\texttt{angular\_spread}. The rest of this section moves from that dataset
level view to the per sample and per pixel evaluation contract.

Each sample is a complete $513\times513$ pixel CKM. The spatial resolution is
\SI{1}{m}\(\times\)\SI{1}{m} per pixel, so each map covers a
\SI{513}{m}\(\times\)\SI{513}{m} square. The UAV transmitter is horizontally
fixed at the centre of the map, while its antenna height \(h_{\mathrm{tx}}\)
varies from sample to sample and is treated as a primary conditioning variable.
The dataset labels this transmitter as a UAV, but the mathematical contract is
an elevated \SI{7.125}{GHz} transmitter with sampled heights above roughly
\SI{10}{m}, so the same surrogate can describe UAVs or other elevated radio
access nodes under the same simulation assumptions.
Every non building pixel is an active receiver location, with the receiver antenna placed exactly at
\SI{1.5}{m} above ground. Building pixels are not receiver positions and are
excluded from the training loss and reported metrics rather than being counted
as easy background pixels. The model never receives a random crop as an
independent training city. Instead, the split is performed at city level. For
the distribution first and final neural models the split uses
\texttt{split\_seed = 42}, with 15\% validation and 15\% test samples after
city assignment. This avoids the most common optimistic error in spatial radio
prediction: training on one part of a city and testing on another visually
similar part of the same city.

The transmitter height samples are not uniformly distributed. They are produced
by the dataset generator through the affine beta mixture
\(h_{\mathrm{design}}=10+490X\), where
\begin{equation}
X\sim 0.75\,\mathrm{Beta}(3,23)+0.25\,\mathrm{Beta}(4,4).
\label{eq:height_beta_mixture}
\end{equation}
For \(a,b>0\), \(\mathrm{Beta}(a,b)\) denotes the distribution with density
\begin{equation}
  f_{a,b}(x)=
  \frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}
  x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1},\qquad 0\le x\le 1,
  \label{eq:beta_density}
\end{equation}
and density zero outside \([0,1]\). Thus the generator first draws a normalized
height variable from the mixture density
\(0.75 f_{3,23}(x)+0.25 f_{4,4}(x)\), then maps it to metres.
However, the finite HDF5 database is the relevant distribution for training
and reporting. Reading the stored \texttt{uav\_height} field for all 16180
samples gives an empirical range of \SIrange{11.61}{477.54}{m}, usually
rounded in the text as about \SIrange{12}{478}{m}; \SI{477.54}{m} is the
largest sampled HDF5 value, not a hard endpoint of the generator distribution.
This is statistically normal: under \(10+490X\),
\(P(h_{\mathrm{tx}}>\SI{478}{m})=3.19\times10^{-5}\) per draw, so only
0.52 such samples are expected in 16180 draws and the probability of drawing
none is about 0.60. A reproducible formulaic version of the realized database
distribution is the \SI{3}{m} empirical density. Let \(\Delta h=\SI{3}{m}\),
\(B_k=[10+3k,\,13+3k)\), and let \(h_i\) be the stored height of
sample \(i\). Then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \hat f_{\Delta h}(h) &= d_k,\quad h\in B_k,\\
  d_k &= \frac{n_k}{N\Delta h},\quad
  n_k=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{h_i\in B_k\},\quad N=16180 .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:height_empirical_histogram}
\end{equation}
When the density is visualized, the plotted curve linearly interpolates
between the bin centre values \((c_k,d_k)\), with \(c_k=11.5+3k\).
Figure~\ref{fig:height_density} shows this realized transmitter height density
directly from the stored HDF5 samples.

The all-database height counts are 3972 samples below \SI{50}{m}, 8456 from
\SIrange{50}{150}{m}, 2490 from \SIrange{150}{300}{m}, and 1262 above
\SI{300}{m}. In the actual training cities, the same bins contain 2676, 5654,

height binned results: the higher altitude bins contain fewer training
examples, so their lower RMSE later in the thesis is evidence that those cases
are physically easier, not that they are better represented in the training
set.

The prior artifacts use the same city holdout contract as the final model:
\texttt{val\_ratio = 0.15}, \texttt{test\_ratio = 0.15}, and
\texttt{split\_seed = 42}. The path loss prior and the spread priors are
fitted on the 10840 training samples. Standalone prior audits can then use
the full held out validation and test pool of 5340 samples, while final
model against prior comparisons use the 2590-sample final test split. This keeps
training cities, validation cities, and final test cities separated throughout
the prior and residual model evaluations.

\section{Shared support maps, visibility masks and morphology}
\label{sec:support_geometry_morphology}

Before any prior is evaluated, the raw map is converted into a common set of
deterministic support maps. This section defines those maps once, because
the LoS attenuation prior, the NLoS attenuation prior, the two spread priors and
the final neural residual model all use the same geometric contract.

Let \(H=W=513\), let \(x=(i,j)\) denote a pixel, and let the transmitter be at
\(x_{\mathrm{tx}}=(i_{\mathrm{tx}},j_{\mathrm{tx}})=(256,256)\). The topology
matrix is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{H\times W},
  \qquad
  T_{ij}= \text{building height at pixel }(i,j)\text{ in metres}.
  \label{eq:topology_matrix}
\end{equation}
The pixel pitch is \(\Delta=\SI{1}{m}\). The receiver height is fixed for the
whole thesis:
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mathrm{rx}}=\SI{1.5}{m}.
  \label{eq:fixed_rx_height}
\end{equation}
The transmitter height \(h_{\mathrm{tx}}\) changes with the sample and is the
only scalar radio geometry input besides the topology map.

The ground receiver mask and building mask are:
\begin{align}
  g_{ij}
  &=
  \mathbf{1}\!\left[T_{ij}\le \epsilon_g\right],
  \qquad \epsilon_g=10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m},
  \label{eq:ground_mask}\\
  b_{ij}
  &=1-g_{ij}.
  \label{eq:building_mask}
\end{align}
Equivalently, \(T(x)\) denotes the building height stored at pixel \(x\),
\(g(x)=1\) means that \(x\) is a ground receiver pixel, and \(b(x)=1\) means
that \(x\) is occupied by a building.
The HDF5 maps use exact zero for ground, while CKMGenerator uses the small
tolerance \(\epsilon_g\) so that image or array inputs with numerical noise are
handled safely. The valid receiver set is:
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_{\mathrm{g}}=\{(i,j):g_{ij}=1\}.
  \label{eq:ground_receiver_set}
\end{equation}
Every pixel in \(\Omega_{\mathrm{g}}\) is a ground receiver. Building pixels are
not receivers, so they are removed from loss functions, metrics and LoS/NLoS
region masks.

For every pixel \(x=(i,j)\), the horizontal distance from the centered
transmitter is:
\begin{equation}
  d_{2D}(x)
  =
  \Delta
  \sqrt{(i-i_{\mathrm{tx}})^2+(j-j_{\mathrm{tx}})^2}.
  \label{eq:d2d_support}
\end{equation}
The direct 3D distance and the LoS path length are the same quantity:
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{LoS}}(x)=d_{3D}(x)
  =
  \sqrt{d_{2D}(x)^2+\left(h_{\mathrm{tx}}-h_{\mathrm{rx}}\right)^2}.
  \label{eq:dlos_equals_d3d}
\end{equation}
The only other 3D path length used by the final LoS prior is the image source
ground reflection distance:
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)
  =
  \sqrt{d_{2D}(x)^2+\left(h_{\mathrm{tx}}+h_{\mathrm{rx}}\right)^2}.
  \label{eq:dref_support}
\end{equation}
Thus \(d_{3D}\) is not a separate feature from \(d_{\mathrm{LoS}}\). The notation
\(d_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is reserved for the reflected two ray path.

The elevation angle is:
\begin{equation}
  \theta(x)
  =
  \arctan2\!\left(h_{\mathrm{tx}}-h_{\mathrm{rx}},\max(d_{2D}(x),1)\right),
  \label{eq:elevation_support}
\end{equation}
with the \(\max(\cdot,1)\) term preventing division by zero below the UAV. The
normalized angle maps are:
\begin{align}
  \theta_{\mathrm{norm}}(x)
  &=
  \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{180\,\theta(x)}{90\pi},0,1\right),
  \label{eq:theta_norm_support}\\
  \theta_{\mathrm{inv}}(x)
  &=1-\theta_{\mathrm{norm}}(x).
  \label{eq:theta_inv_support}
\end{align}
Therefore \(\theta_{\mathrm{norm}}\) is high for near overhead links, while
\(\theta_{\mathrm{inv}}\) is high for grazing links that pass through more urban
structure.

The LoS mask is treated as a support map, not as a target radio map. In the
HDF5 experiments the stored CKM mask is used directly and then restricted to
ground receivers:
\begin{align}
  m_{\mathrm{LoS}}(x)
  &=
  g(x)\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[m_{\mathrm{LoS,HDF5}}(x)>0.5\right],
  \label{eq:los_mask_from_hdf5}\\
  m_{\mathrm{NLoS}}(x)
  &=
  g(x)\,\left(1-\mathbf{1}\!\left[m_{\mathrm{LoS,HDF5}}(x)>0.5\right]\right).
  \label{eq:nlos_mask_from_hdf5}
\end{align}
This ground multiplication is essential: \(1-m_{\mathrm{LoS,HDF5}}\) alone would
incorrectly mark building pixels as NLoS receivers. When CKMGenerator receives
an input that has no provided LoS mask, it generates one from the topology and
transmitter height by ray casting. For a candidate ground receiver \(x=(i,j)\),
define:
\begin{equation}
  L_x
  =
  \max\!\left(
  1,
  \left\lceil
  \frac{\max(|i-i_{\mathrm{tx}}|,|j-j_{\mathrm{tx}}|)}{s}
  \right\rceil
  \right),
  \qquad s=\SI{1}{pixel}
  \label{eq:los_raycast_steps}
\end{equation}
for the default generator setting. The implementation enforces
\(s\ge \SI{0.25}{pixel}\) if the user requests a finer step. For
\(\ell=1,\ldots,L_x-1\), the sampled point on the horizontal ray is:
\begin{align}
  t_{\ell,x} &= \frac{\ell}{L_x},
  \label{eq:ray_t}\\
  q_{\ell,x}
  &=
  \left(
  \mathrm{round}\!\left(i_{\mathrm{tx}}+(i-i_{\mathrm{tx}})t_{\ell,x}\right),
  \mathrm{round}\!\left(j_{\mathrm{tx}}+(j-j_{\mathrm{tx}})t_{\ell,x}\right)
  \right).
  \label{eq:ray_sample_cell}
\end{align}
The height of the ideal straight segment from the UAV to the receiver is:
\begin{equation}
  z_{\ell,x}
  =
  h_{\mathrm{tx}}
  +
  \left(h_{\mathrm{rx}}-h_{\mathrm{tx}}\right)t_{\ell,x}.
  \label{eq:ray_line_height}
\end{equation}
With default clearance \(c_{\mathrm{clear}}=0\), the generated obstruction flag is:
\begin{equation}
  o(x)
  =
  \mathbf{1}\!\left[
  \exists\,\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L_x-1\}:
  q_{\ell,x}\notin\{x_{\mathrm{tx}},x\}
  \ \mathrm{and}\
  T(q_{\ell,x})+c_{\mathrm{clear}}\ge z_{\ell,x}
  \right].
  \label{eq:ray_obstruction}
\end{equation}
The generated masks are then:
\begin{align}
  m_{\mathrm{LoS,gen}}(x)&=g(x)\,[1-o(x)],
  \label{eq:los_mask_generated}\\
  m_{\mathrm{NLoS,gen}}(x)&=g(x)\,o(x).
  \label{eq:nlos_mask_generated}
\end{align}
In words, for each candidate ground receiver CKMGenerator walks along the
horizontal segment from the centered UAV to that receiver, skips the transmitter
and receiver cells themselves, and checks whether any sampled building reaches
or crosses the height of the straight aerial link. If that happens the receiver
is generated as NLoS; otherwise it is generated as LoS. Building pixels remain
outside both masks because both generated masks are multiplied by \(g(x)\).
CKMGenerator uses the provided mask in automatic mode when it exists; otherwise
it uses \eqref{eq:los_mask_generated}. The generated ray casting mask is useful
for topology only deployment, but it is not expected to be bit identical to the
stored CKM mask because the original simulator and the lightweight generator do
not share the full ray tracing engine.

The local morphology features are also computed before any prior is evaluated.
For \(k\in\{15,41\}\), let \(\mathrm{BoxMean}_k\) be a reflected padding
\(k\times k\) average:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BoxMean}_k(f)(i,j)
  =
  \frac{1}{k^2}
  \sum_{u=i-p}^{i+p}
  \sum_{v=j-p}^{j+p}
  f_{\mathrm{ref}}(u,v),
  \qquad p=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,
  \label{eq:boxmean_support}
\end{equation}
where \(f_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is \(f\) reflected at the map boundary. The final
prior bundle uses:
\begin{align}
  \rho_k(x)
  &=
  \mathrm{BoxMean}_k(b)(x),
  \label{eq:rho_support}\\
  h_k(x)
  &=
  \frac{\mathrm{BoxMean}_k(T\odot b)(x)}{H_{\mathrm{norm}}},
  \qquad H_{\mathrm{norm}}=\SI{90}{m},
  \label{eq:h_support}\\
  n_k(x)
  &=
  \mathrm{BoxMean}_k(m_{\mathrm{NLoS}})(x).
  \label{eq:n_support}
\end{align}
\(\rho_k\) is local building coverage, \(h_k\) is density weighted local
building height, and \(n_k\) is the local fraction of the window that is both
ground and NLoS. For the spread prior, three additional \(41\times41\)
morphology terms are used:
\begin{align}
  b_{41}(x)
  &=
  \frac{\mathrm{BoxMean}_{41}\!\left(\max(T-h_{\mathrm{rx}},0)\odot b\right)(x)}
       {H_{\mathrm{norm}}},
  \label{eq:blocker_41_support}\\
  c_{41}(x)
  &=
  \frac{\mathrm{BoxMean}_{41}\!\left(\max(h_{\mathrm{tx}}-T,0)\odot b\right)(x)}
       {H_{\mathrm{norm}}},
  \label{eq:clearance_41_support}\\
  t_{41}(x)
  &=
  \mathrm{BoxMean}_{41}\!\left(\mathbf{1}[T>h_{\mathrm{tx}}]\odot b\right)(x).
  \label{eq:taller_41_support}
\end{align}
Here \(b_{41}\) measures local blocker depth above the receiver, \(c_{41}\)
measures the UAV height margin over nearby buildings, and \(t_{41}\) measures
the local area fraction occupied by buildings taller than the UAV.

Two topology regime systems are used because the calibrated attenuation prior
and the spread prior were fitted with different levels of granularity. Both are
computed once per map from:
\begin{align}
  \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}
  &=
  \frac{1}{HW}\sum_x b(x),
  \label{eq:map_density_support}\\
  \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}
  &=
  \frac{\sum_x T(x)b(x)}
       {\max(\sum_x b(x),1)}.
  \label{eq:map_height_support}
\end{align}
The three class path loss regime is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{class}_{\mathrm{PL}} =
\begin{cases}
\texttt{dense\_highrise}, & \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}\ge0.2549\ \text{or}\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}\ge\SI{15.95}{m},\\
\texttt{open\_lowrise}, & \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}\le0.1957\ \text{and}\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}\le\SI{10.91}{m},\\
\texttt{mixed\_midrise}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:topology_class_pl_support}
\end{equation}
The six class spread and reporting regime is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{class}_{\mathrm{SP}} =
\begin{cases}
\texttt{open\_sparse\_lowrise}, & \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}\le0.12,\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}\le\SI{12}{m},\\
\texttt{open\_sparse\_vertical}, & \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}\le0.12,\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}>\SI{12}{m},\\
\texttt{dense\_block\_midrise}, & \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}\ge0.28,\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}\le\SI{28}{m},\\
\texttt{dense\_block\_highrise}, & \bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}\ge0.28,\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}>\SI{28}{m},\\
\texttt{mixed\_compact\_lowrise}, & 0.12<\bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}<0.28,\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}\le\SI{12}{m},\\
\texttt{mixed\_compact\_midrise}, & 0.12<\bar\rho_{\mathrm{map}}<0.28,\ \bar h_{\mathrm{map}}>\SI{12}{m}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:topology_class_sp_support}
\end{equation}
The shared calibration height key is:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ant\_bin}(h_{\mathrm{tx}})=
\begin{cases}
\texttt{low\_ant}, & h_{\mathrm{tx}}\le\SI{58.12}{m},\\
\texttt{mid\_ant}, & \SI{58.12}{m}<h_{\mathrm{tx}}\le\SI{103.85}{m},\\
\texttt{high\_ant}, & h_{\mathrm{tx}}>\SI{103.85}{m}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ant_bin_support}
\end{equation}
This calibration key is different from the four broader height intervals used
only for result reporting: below \SI{50}{m}, \SIrange{50}{150}{m},
\SIrange{150}{300}{m}, and above \SI{300}{m}. Finally, the normalized scalar
height feature used in the spread vector is:
\begin{equation}
  h_{\mathrm{norm}}
  =
  \mathrm{clip}\!\left(
  \frac{\log(1+\max(h_{\mathrm{tx}}-h_{\mathrm{rx}},0))}{\log(401)},
  0,1
  \right).
  \label{eq:h_norm_support}
\end{equation}

All RMSE values in this thesis are pixel-weighted over valid ground pixels:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE}
  =
  \sqrt{
  \frac{\sum_{s,i,j} m_s(i,j)\left(\hat y_s(i,j)-y_s(i,j)\right)^2}
       {\sum_{s,i,j} m_s(i,j)}
  }.
  \label{eq:rmse}
\end{equation}
This is stricter than averaging per sample RMSEs because large valid maps
contribute proportionally to the number of receiver pixels. The same evaluation
contract is used for channel attenuation, delay spread and angular spread. For
the two spread targets, every non building pixel is also a receiver pixel in the
CKM ground truth, so the reported spread RMSE is simply the dense prediction
compared with the dense ray-traced ground truth over all valid ground receivers.

\section[Channel Attenuation Prior]{Channel Attenuation Prior and Residual Prediction}
\label{sec:path_loss_prior_detail}

The CKM dataset stores the attenuation target under the code name
\texttt{path\_loss}. The final attenuation prior is therefore built from
physical path loss formulas, but its dense output is reported in the thesis as
channel attenuation. The prior coefficients and calibration tables are fixed
before neural training and have two branches.

The LoS branch starts from free space path loss, adds a coherent two ray
ground reflection correction, and then applies height dependent bias and radial
residual calibration. This captures the ring like structure visible around the
centred transmitter. The NLoS branch starts from a blocked link propagation
estimate and calibrates it with local morphology, obstruction and elevation
features. Both branches are fitted only on training cities. At inference, the
explicit LoS/NLoS support mask selects the branch at each ground receiver pixel,
producing the fixed attenuation prior map used by \textsc{HARP-Net CKM}.

Chapter~\ref{chap:results} reports the quantitative prior audit. The full
equations, feature inventory and fitting details are included below.

\section{Delay and Angular Spread Prior and Residual Prediction}
\label{sec:spread_prior_detail}

Angular spread uses the shared spread prior described below. Its final
prediction is the fixed angular spread prior plus the bounded residual
correction predicted by \textsc{HARP-Net CKM}. The target is reported in
degrees and is evaluated only on valid ground receiver pixels.

Delay spread uses the same shared spread prior machinery, but it is evaluated
in nanoseconds and is more sensitive to rare high delay pixels. The model
therefore uses the prior as a stable baseline and learns only a bounded
correction.

For both spread targets, the prior is fitted in \(\log(1+y)\) space because
the maps are nonnegative, strongly skewed, and contain near zero LoS mass plus
heavier NLoS tails. The raw spread estimate combines propagation state,
distance, elevation and local morphology. A train only ridge calibration then
specializes this estimate by metric, topology class, LoS/NLoS state and
transmitter height bin, with fallback keys for sparse regimes. The calibrated
prior is inverse transformed to native units before it is compared with
ray-traced ground truth or passed to \textsc{HARP-Net CKM}.

\section{Training and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:try80_detail}

\textsc{HARP-Net CKM} receives nine spatial channels plus a separate scalar
transmitter height conditioning path. The channels are the normalized topology
map, LoS mask, NLoS mask, ground mask, combined attenuation prior, LoS
attenuation prior, calibrated NLoS attenuation branch, delay spread prior and
angular spread prior. The scalar height is encoded with sinusoidal features and
injected through FiLM layers rather than copied as a constant image.

The network is a shared GroupNorm U-Net with task specific residual heads. For
each target and visibility region, the head predicts bounded residual
components around the fixed prior baseline. The probabilistic parameterization is kept
because it represents residual tails during training, but the reported maps are
deterministic: channel attenuation is read in native dB space, while delay
spread and angular spread are predicted in \(\log(1+y)\) model space and then
mapped back to ns or degrees. The explicit LoS/NLoS masks select the final
regional branch at each valid ground receiver pixel.

Training uses the same city holdout split, receiver mask and pixel weighted
RMSE contract defined at the start of this chapter. The selected final stage is
RMSE/MAE oriented, with small anchor and prior guard terms so that the residual
model improves the prior baselines without freely overwriting them. The full
architecture diagram, residual assembly equations and loss family are included
below.
